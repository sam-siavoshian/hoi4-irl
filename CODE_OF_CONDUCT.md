# Code of conduct

Home Front is a small project. People show up here in their spare time, and some of them are showing a
stranger their code for the first time. These are the house rules.

## What we expect

- **Be kind and direct.** Say what you think about the code or the idea, and say it the way you would
  to a colleague you respect.
- **Assume good faith.** A blunt message is usually a rushed one. Ask before you take offence.
- **Criticise the work, not the person.** "This breaks replay determinism" is useful. "You clearly
  did not read the code" is not.
- **Make room.** Answer beginners' questions without sighing. Everyone was new once.
- **Take no for an answer.** Maintainers may decline a change that does not fit the project. You are
  free to fork; that is what the licence is for.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments about anyone's identity, background, body, beliefs or
  experience level.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome personal attention.
- Publishing someone else's private information.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or continuing after being asked to stop.

## About the theme

Home Front uses a 1936 war map as a metaphor for one person's own life. It is not a place for real-world
political or nationalist arguments, for glorifying actual violence, or for hostility towards any
country or people that happens to appear on the map. Keep discussion about the software.

## Where this applies

Issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space run by the project.

## If something goes wrong

Contact the maintainers privately. The simplest route is the repository's
[private report form](https://github.com/sam-siavoshian/homefront-map/security/advisories/new) (mark it as a
conduct report), or a direct message to a maintainer listed on the repository. Reports are kept
confidential.

Maintainers will read the report, ask for context if needed, and respond in proportion: a quiet word,
an edited or removed comment, a temporary block, or a permanent one for serious or repeated behaviour.
Maintainers are held to the same rules as everyone else.
